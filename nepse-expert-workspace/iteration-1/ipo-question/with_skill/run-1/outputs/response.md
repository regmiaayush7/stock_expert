### 2. Why 1000 kitta does not help

In both oversubscribed issues (Appolo 38x, Beni about 30x) every winner got exactly 10 kitta by lottery. A 1000-kitta form has the same odds as a 10-kitta form. It only ties up Rs 1,00,000 instead of Rs 1,000 until allotment, about 10 days after close in both cases. Bigger forms only matter if the issue is barely oversubscribed. SEBON's 10-unit floor per applicant is from a 2017 report (old, verify). I could not confirm that 1000 is the current maximum, so check the prospectus.

### 5. Strongest case against

- The expected gain is under Rs 100 per application, and you wait weeks for it.
- The pattern comes from a 2026 run of thin-float listings. Ten pops in a row is exactly when to be careful.
- Supply may change. SEBON has a draft "General Eligibility Guidelines for Public Issuance, 2026". The hydropower association objects to it. Rules may tighten or loosen, and I do not know which.
- Your 10 kitta may be a separate decision from the listing pop. At Rs 400 to 600, this stock would be valued at four to six times par, and I have not analysed that.

It loses only on cost: the downside before allotment is just blocked cash, so applying is still fine.

### 6. What would change my call

- The prospectus shows a project far from complete (the 65% construction rule is how hydros qualify), heavy debt, or net worth per share below Rs 100.
- SEBON or NEPSE changes the listing range, or the market drops sharply before listing. I saw NEPSE at about 2,634 on 16 Oct (aggregator, unverified), against a 52-week range of 2,469 to 2,970.
- The applicant count turns out much higher than 25 lakh.

### Bias flag

Recency and FOMO: ten straight pops make Rs 300 feel certain. Max-kitta thinking: more kitta feels like better odds. The better question is whether you want 10 kitta of this company at Rs 100 if it never pops.

- Anything in the week of 19 to 23 Oct (markets looked shut; I do not know why).
