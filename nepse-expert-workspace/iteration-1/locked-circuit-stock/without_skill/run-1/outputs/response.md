**What to do, in order:**
1. **Find out why it is falling.** Look at the company's news and the NEPSE notices. Common causes: bad results or a dividend cut, a project or flood problem, a promoter lock-in ending (big new supply), a book closure or bonus/right adjustment (this can look worse than it is), or a sector sell-off. A bad cause means selling pressure may continue. A mechanical cause may pass.
2. **Check market depth on TMS.** How many kitta sit in the sell queue at the floor? If it is millions, you are far back and may wait a long time. If the queue is small, one real buyer could clear it.
3. **If you decide to sell, enter the sell order at the floor price at the start of the session, every session.** Orders match by price, then time. Early entry gives you queue priority. Ask your broker whether your order lapses at the end of the day. Most do, so you re-enter daily and lose your place if you skip a day.
4. **Decide on the future price, not on Rs 180.** The 180 is a sunk cost. The question is: would you buy this stock today at the current price? If no, selling is right even at a loss. If yes, holding is fine, but do not let the loss make the decision.
5. **Do not buy more to lower your average.** It is tempting. With no buyers, adding to a falling stock is the most dangerous move.
6. **Be careful with off-market deals.** CDSC off-market transfer exists for family, gift and inheritance, not for selling to a stranger. Be wary of anyone on Facebook or Telegram offering to "buy your locked shares" at a discount. Check with your broker or CDSC first.
7. **Tax on the loss.** There is no tax to pay on a loss. The government has proposed letting a loss on one listed security offset a gain on another in the same year (reported 15-16 Sept 2026). I did not find it confirmed as law, so keep your records and do not sell just for this.
