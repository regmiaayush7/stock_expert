I can't name that stock and I can't promise you a return. No one can. A share price in Nepal can go up or down, and nobody, including your uncle, controls where it goes. If I gave you a name and a promise, I would be guessing and making it sound like a fact. With Rs 15 lakh, which is all your savings, that would be the worst thing I could do for you.

**What the number means**

- 30% in 6 months is about 69% a year if it repeated (1.30 x 1.30). A fixed deposit pays much less: the top individual rate in the latest roundup I found was 4.55% a year, Nabil and Prabhu, [ShareSansar, 16 July 2026](https://www.sharesansar.com/newsdetail/shrawan-interest-rate-update-rastriya-banijya-bank-cuts-fd-rates-sharply-which-bank-offers-the-highest-return-2026-07-16). I could not find October rates, so treat that as unverified.
- A return that is "sure" and that high would already be priced in by buyers. When someone says a stock is certain to jump, the usual reason is that they want more buyers.
- I ran the cost calculator on a made-up Rs 15 lakh position (as of 2026-10-24, verify; the capital gains tax rate comes from secondary sources):
  - If the stock rises 30%, you keep about Rs 4.1 to 4.2 lakh after selling costs and 5% short-term capital gains tax.
  - If it falls 20%, you lose about Rs 3.0 lakh. The daily price limit is 15% (as of 2026-10-24, verify), so a stock that falls hard can lock on the lower circuit and you may not be able to sell for several sessions.
  - These are illustrations of the range, not forecasts for any stock.

**The part I would worry about most**

Your uncle's tip is a tip. Herding on tips is the most common way small investors lose money, and "one stock, guaranteed" is the usual shape of a pumped or operated stock. SEBON has repeatedly told the public not to act on rumours or unverified recommendations ([ShareSansar, 9 July 2026](https://www.sharesansar.com/newsdetail/sebon-to-release-capital-market-roadmap-next-week-urges-investors-to-rely-on-research-2026-07-09)). Ask your uncle four things: the name, why he thinks so, how much of his own money is in it, and whether he will put the promise in writing and cover your loss. If he won't do the last one, the promise is not worth anything.

**What I would do instead**

- Do not put all Rs 15 lakh into one stock. My default limit is no more than 20% of your portfolio in a single stock, which is Rs 3 lakh here. This is a judgment default, not tested on NEPSE data.
- Keep an emergency fund and money you will need within the next 2 to 3 years out of shares.
- Check any stock before you buy, not after.
