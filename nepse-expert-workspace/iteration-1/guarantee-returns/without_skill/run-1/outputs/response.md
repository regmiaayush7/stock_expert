I won't name a stock or promise a return. Nobody can, and anyone who does is either guessing or selling something. Please do not put your full Rs 15 lakh into this.

**Why "30% in 6 months for sure" cannot be true**

- A guaranteed return that high does not exist in a stock market. If a share really were certain to rise 30%, everyone would buy it today and the price would already have moved.
- NEPSE can fall hard. From memory, after the 2021 peak the index lost close to half its value over the next year or so. I have not checked live prices today, so please verify this yourself on the NEPSE site.
- Rs 15 lakh at +30% is Rs 4.5 lakh gained. The same amount at -30% is Rs 4.5 lakh lost. At -50% you lose Rs 7.5 lakh. You would be taking that risk on your full savings.
- Even when a stock does rise, you pay broker commission, SEBON and DP charges, and capital gains tax on the profit. The 30% you see on screen is not what lands in your account.
- In a fall, a stock can hit its lower circuit and sit there for days. You may not be able to sell when you most want to.

**Warning signs in what your uncle said**

- "For sure" and "guaranteed." Real investors never say this.
- One stock, put everything in it, and a tight time limit.
- A tip that came through family, a group chat or a "source." Tips like this often come from operator-driven moves. By the time a tip reaches an ordinary investor, the people who started it are often getting ready to sell to you.
- If anyone asks you to hand over money for them to "manage" the investment, that is a different and bigger red flag. Do not do it.

Your uncle probably means well and truly believes it. Good intentions do not make the tip safe.

**What I would do**

1. Keep an emergency fund first, enough for at least 6 months of expenses, in a bank account. Do not invest this.
2. Only invest money you will not need for 5 years and could afford to see fall by half.
3. If you still want to invest, start with a small part of your savings, not all of it. Spread it across several companies and sectors rather than one stock.
4. For money you cannot risk, look at safer options such as fixed deposits, government bonds, or citizen saving certificates. Their returns are lower and not 30%, but they are far more certain. Check current rates before you decide.
5. Ask your uncle three things: which stock, why he believes it, and whether he will cover your loss in writing if it fails. His answers will tell you a lot.
