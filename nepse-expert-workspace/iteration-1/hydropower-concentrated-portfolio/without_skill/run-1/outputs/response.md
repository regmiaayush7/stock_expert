**Why this is risky**

The four hydro names are one bet, not four. They share the same rain, floods, grid and spill limits, NEA/PPA rules and investor mood. It already showed in August: after the Rasuwa flood, the hydropower index fell over 2.5% in a day and Chilime fell 7.4% ([ShareSansar](https://www.sharesansar.com/newsdetail/hydropower-index-falls-more-than-25-as-rasuwa-flood-damages-hydropower-infrastructure-2026-08-26)). Your two banks do not offset that: they are only 15%.

If the hydro index fell 25%, you would lose about Rs 3.3 lakh. That is about five times your whole unrealised gain. You do not need to be wrong about hydro for that to happen. One bad monsoon year or one rule change is enough.

**What I would do**

1. Do not sell everything. Do not sell in a panic. You are over a year in, so you pay the lower long-term capital gains tax.
2. Trim NHPC first. It is your biggest weight and your best gain. Sell about 1,000 kitta in 2 to 3 tranches over a couple of weeks. Rough net is Rs 2.5 lakh after broker fees and tax.
3. Trim AKJCL by about 500 kitta (roughly Rs 1.7 lakh). It is only +4.8%, so selling costs you little tax. Or trim CHCL instead if you prefer.
4. After steps 2 and 3, hydro falls from 85% to about 56% of the portfolio. That is still a heavy hydro tilt, and it respects your belief. Go lower later if you can.
5. UPPER: do not sell just because it is down 20%. The loss is already there either way. Ask one question instead: "If I had Rs 2.9 lakh cash today, would I buy UPPER at 193 with this weight?" If yes, hold. If no, sell, and treat the loss as the price of leaving. Do not average down. Your cash is too small for it, and it would add more hydro.
6. Put the proceeds (about Rs 4.3 lakh) outside hydro. Options are other sectors (insurance, a bank or two), a mutual fund or a fixed deposit. Keep 10 to 15% in cash so you are never forced to sell in a crash. Also keep a real emergency fund outside the market, not the Rs 25,000.
7. Check for a book closure date on each stock before you sell. Selling too early can lose you a dividend or bonus.

**Tax and cost notes**

I used 3.75% in my numbers. The gap is about Rs 100 on a 1,000 kitta NHPC sale. Confirm the rate with your broker.
- Do not count on offsetting the UPPER loss against gains. Loss offset was only part of a proposal. I could not confirm it is law.
- Broker commission, SEBON fee and DP charge add roughly 0.4% on each sale.

**Counter-case**

Hydro has been strong for you, and trimming a winner like NHPC means paying tax and maybe missing more upside. That is true. The point is that you can be right on the direction and still be hurt by sizing. Trimming to about 56% keeps most of the upside.
